# Nickelate superconductivity: compare conditions before comparing temperatures

**Exploratory, not gold — agent_reported scientific draft.** Research question: Which pairing mechanisms and structural conditions explain superconductivity across nickelate families, and which experimental results are genuinely comparable?

<a id="findings"></a>
## Concise findings

The inspected evidence supports a **family-, preparation- and measurement-specific** account, not a single pooled nickelate transition temperature. A bilayer film report separates resistive onset **45 K**, BKT-like **9 K**, and magnetic **8.5 K** scales [4]; a Sm-substituted bilayer crystal report separates onset **92 K** and zero resistance **73 K** at **21.6 GPa**, and a Meissner transition **60 K** at **20.6 GPa** [5]. These are not interchangeable estimates of one quantity. Positive pressurized trilayer results and a negative differently prepared tetragonal sample series constrain simplistic “structure alone” stories [6,7]. The evidence inspected here **does not select a unique microscopic pairing mechanism** [7,9,10].

<a id="rivals"></a>
## Competing explanations and contradictions

**Structure versus preparation.** N1 links a positive high-pressure result to Fmmm and orbital changes; N4 obtains ambient-pressure film signatures with substrate strain and a different composition [1,4]. Neither establishes strain and hydrostatic pressure as equivalent tuning axes. N5 explicitly reports that more than one high-pressure structural phase can support SC [5]. The negative tetragonal microcrystals in N6 and positive pressurized crystals in N7 are adverse evidence against “the tetragonal label guarantees SC,” not a controlled contradiction at identical stoichiometry, growth history and pressure/temperature calibration [6,7]. N6's stronger claim about necessity of density-wave order is not adopted here as a demonstrated causal result.

**Intrinsic superconductivity versus minority paths.** Resistive onset, zero resistance, and magnetic response are distinct observations in N4/N5 [4,5]. They should be checked jointly, not collapsed into a headline maximum. N8 itself names low volume fraction, non-percolation and intergrowths as an alternative origin [8]; this limits an intrinsic homogeneous-trilayer interpretation. No shielding fraction is inferred where none was reported.

**Pairing mechanisms.** N7's AFM-superexchange interpretation is theoretical, N9 predicts multiple infinite-layer states as model parameters change, and N10 explores several bilayer instabilities while assuming magnetic correlations [7,9,10]. These establish hypotheses worth discriminating, not selection of one by transition temperature. Phase-sensitive gap/sign information and well-characterized comparable samples would be needed for such discrimination; this dossier contains no audited direct symmetry measurement that settles the alternatives [9,10].

<a id="replication"></a>
## Replication, uncertainty and source status

Different author lists or compositions do not prove independent replication of one sample class. N4's three temperatures and N5's three measurement rows are within-paper evidence, not independent groups. N6/N7 even share Peng and Zeng among authors; shared people do not imply identical data, but they preclude treating different papers as automatically independent. N3 and N2 have different authors/compositions; they demonstrate breadth in the source set, not a matched reproduction. No matched composition/phase/substrate/preparation/calibration/Tc-definition independent replication was verified in the inspected abstracts [2–7].

Pressure calibration, pressure media/hydrostaticity, current/field settings, thermometer/calibration uncertainty, transition widths, sample IDs and dataset matching remain unavailable where the abstracts do not state them. Do not infer ruby or diamond calibration, zero error, zero pressure, a shielding fraction or sample homogeneity. The numerical Tc claims above are authors’ reports, not re-derived measurements [1,4–7]. Primary title/authors/date/identifier and passage checks are saved as short attributable excerpts in [primary-checks.json](primary-checks.json); machine-readable claims and evidence-row links are in [citation-audit.json](citation-audit.json). Online record and issue dates differ for N2 (online 2021; publisher citation issue 2022); its identifier, not a guessed year, fixes identity. Journal-linked preprint abstracts were inspected as preprints, without assuming journal text equivalence. Abstract version histories are not a complete correction/retraction registry clearance.

<a id="conclusion"></a>
## Evidence-proportionate conclusion

**What is demonstrated in the inspected records:** authors report transport/magnetic signatures in several distinct nickelate systems; particular film and pressure studies provide multiple, unequal transition definitions [1–5,7]. **What is interpretation:** structural/orbital correlations, BKT-like behavior, AFM pairing explanations and particular symmetry predictions [1,4,7,9,10]. **What remains open here:** exact independent replication under matched conditions, intrinsic-phase attribution for minority signatures, and a unique pairing mechanism [6,8–10].

Do not pool these temperatures, rank families by an unqualified maximum, or infer pairing glue from Tc. The useful result is a comparability map and a constrained inconclusive mechanism verdict, not a universal nickelate theory. This is selected abstract-led research, not an exhaustive review; adverse results and missing conditions reduce the conclusion, not the honesty of workflow completion. No PDF/full-text acquisition, experimental reanalysis, human scientific acceptance, privacy approval or package export was performed. Provenance checks establish artifact integrity/run linkage, not scientific truth.
